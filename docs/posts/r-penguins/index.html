Introduction

In this post, I explore the Palmer Penguins dataset using R. I will compare body mass across penguin species and investigate how bill measurements differ between species.

Data: Palmer Penguins, Palmer Station Antarctica LTER.

The Palmer Penguins dataset was collected by Dr. Kristen Gorman and the Palmer Station Long Term Ecological Research program. The dataset is available under the CC0 licence.

Preparing the data

For this analysis, I focus on species, body mass, bill length, and bill depth. I remove rows that have missing values in any of these variables.

analysis_penguins <- penguins[
  complete.cases(
    penguins[, c("species", "body_mass_g", "bill_length_mm", "bill_depth_mm")]
  ),
  c("species", "body_mass_g", "bill_length_mm", "bill_depth_mm")
]

Comparing species

Next, I calculate the average body mass, bill length, and bill depth for each penguin species.

species_summary <- aggregate(
  cbind(body_mass_g, bill_length_mm, bill_depth_mm) ~ species,
  data = analysis_penguins,
  FUN = mean
)

species_summary[, -1] <- round(species_summary[, -1], 1)

species_summary
    species body_mass_g bill_length_mm bill_depth_mm
1    Adelie      3700.7           38.8          18.3
2 Chinstrap      3733.1           48.8          18.4
3    Gentoo      5076.0           47.5          15.0

The summary shows that the three species differ in their typical body measurements. Gentoo penguins have the greatest average body mass, while the species also show noticeable differences in average bill length and bill depth.

Body mass by species

To see the differences in body mass more clearly, I create a boxplot for the three penguin species.

boxplot(
  body_mass_g ~ species,
  data = analysis_penguins,
  xlab = "Species",
  ylab = "Body mass (g)",
  main = "Body mass by penguin species"
)

The figure shows clear differences in body mass among the species. Gentoo penguins tend to have higher body mass values than Adelie and Chinstrap penguins.

Relationship between bill measurements

Finally, I calculate the correlation between bill length and bill depth separately for each species.

bill_correlations <- sapply(
  split(analysis_penguins, analysis_penguins$species),
  function(group) {
    cor(group$bill_length_mm, group$bill_depth_mm)
  }
)

round(bill_correlations, 3)
   Adelie Chinstrap    Gentoo 
    0.391     0.654     0.643

These values show how bill length and bill depth are related within each species. Calculating the correlations separately avoids mixing the three species together when examining this relationship.

Conclusion

This analysis used R to explore differences in body and bill measurements among Palmer penguin species. After checking for missing data and preparing a complete dataset, I compared average measurements across species and visualized the distribution of body mass.

The results show clear differences among Adelie, Chinstrap, and Gentoo penguins. Gentoo penguins are heavier on average, and the species also differ in their bill measurements. The analysis demonstrates how data cleaning, summary statistics, visualization, and correlation can be used to explore a biological dataset in R.
